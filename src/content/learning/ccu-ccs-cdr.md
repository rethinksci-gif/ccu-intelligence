**CCU** uses captured carbon dioxide in a product or process. **CCS** places captured CO₂ into storage. **CDR** removes CO₂ from the atmosphere and stores it durably. The origin and final fate of the carbon determine whether removal is possible; the presence of capture equipment does not settle the question. [DOE overview](https://www.energy.gov/carbon-capture-utilization-storage)

## Follow the carbon, not the label

| Outcome | What must be established? |
| --- | --- |
| Carbon avoidance | Emissions are lower than a defined counterfactual. |
| Fossil substitution | A fossil feedstock is displaced, without assuming net removal. |
| Temporary use | Carbon stays in the product for a limited time and may be released. |
| Durable storage | Retention and reversal risks support the claimed duration. |
| Atmospheric removal | Atmospheric origin, durable storage and net lifecycle removal are demonstrated. |

Fuel synthesis can recycle carbon, but combustion releases it again. Mineralization can retain carbon longer, yet upstream processing can offset the benefit. Product use alone therefore does not establish a net climate benefit. [IEA, *Putting CO₂ to Use*](https://www.iea.org/reports/putting-co2-to-use)

**Analyst reading rule:** ask separately where the carbon came from, what energy was used, what product was displaced, and where the carbon ends up. Treat missing answers as uncertainty.
