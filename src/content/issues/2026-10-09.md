## Technology Spotlight

**Methanol is a shared product, not a single technology.** The electrochemical preprint lead should be assessed separately from the hydrogen-based project records. An electrochemical study needs evidence on product identification, charge efficiency, carbon balance, electrode stability and the energy required to separate dilute products. A reported product name alone cannot establish an economically viable process.

For the conventional hydrogenation balance, CO₂ + 3 H₂ → CH₃OH + H₂O implies approximately 1.374 tonnes of CO₂ and 0.189 tonnes of H₂ per tonne of methanol before losses. Those are theoretical reactant requirements, not measured plant consumption. The existing site calculator illustrates how assumed feed utilisation and purchased-hydrogen price change a partial cost estimate. A recycle loop does not remove the need to account for purge losses, feed purification and product separation. [Renewable-methanol reference](https://www.irena.org/publications/2021/Jan/Innovation-Outlook-Renewable-Methanol)

No catalyst recipe, operating temperature, pressure or demonstrated TRL is assigned to the preprint from its title. No generic pathway-wide readiness score is substituted for evidence from a named installation.

## Economics & Climate Reality Check

The existing illustrative calculator gives approximately **USD 768 per tonne of methanol** using assumed CO₂ at USD 80/t, H₂ at USD 3/kg, electricity at USD 50/MWh, process electricity of 1 MWh/t, and utilisation of 90% for CO₂ and 95% for H₂. These are scenario inputs, not October market quotations. Purchased hydrogen already incorporates its production cost; adding electrolyser electricity again would double-count it.

The functional unit is one tonne of methanol. The boundary includes purchased feedstocks and process electricity only. Geography is unspecified. Capital recovery, fixed operating costs, heat, water, logistics and margins are excluded. The comparison with an assumed USD 350/t fossil benchmark is a sensitivity exercise, not a forecast or a minimum selling price. [Assessment guidance](https://assessccus.globalco2initiative.org/)

No lifecycle advantage is calculated in this issue. That would require compatible electricity, hydrogen, carbon-source and end-of-life assumptions against a defined reference system. Methanol combustion releases its carbon; using captured CO₂ is not automatically durable carbon removal. The draft therefore does not repeat company percentage-saving or “carbon-neutral” claims.
