## Conversion technology

### Integrated Ni single-atom electrode reaches 0.760 A cm−2 CO in aqueous CO2 electrolysis

Researchers report that a hierarchically porous integrated electrode with Ni single-atom-anchored CNT arrays on carbon fiber paper achieved a CO partial current density of 0.760 A cm−2 at 0.5 MPa CO2 in a pressurized zero-gap electrolyzer fed by aqueous electrolyte, a 3.67-fold enhancement over a conventional layered electrode. Operando analyses indicate the integrated structure reduced concentration polarization by 37%, according to the abstract. — [OpenAlex, 2026-10-08](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.6c05782) (abstract only)

*Abstract only; durability, cell area and energy efficiency not reported.*

### Interfacial alkalization imposes ~0.6 V net penalty on CO2-to-C2+ MEA electrolysis

An analysis of CO2 electroreduction reports that cation-induced shielding and local proton depletion at industrial current densities cause an operational thermodynamic penalty of 0.83 V for ethylene or ethanol in zero-gap membrane electrode assembly cells, leaving a persistent net thermodynamic voltage penalty of about 0.6 V. The authors conclude that the sequential CO2-to-CO then CO-to-C2+ cascade is currently the most robust industrial strategy to bypass this energy floor. — [OpenAlex, 2026-09-29](https://doi.org/10.1021/acselectrochem.6c00222) (abstract only)

*Review abstract; no experimental energy efficiency or cost data provided.*

### Zn-doped Cu2O nanocubes achieve 82.7% C2+ Faradaic efficiency at 800 mA cm−2

A Zn-doped Cu2O nanocube catalyst synthesized by a one-pot method achieved 82.7% Faradaic efficiency for C2+ products and 44.0% ethanol Faradaic efficiency at 800 mA cm−2 in a flow cell, according to an abstract. The Zn dopant is reported to stabilize Cu+ species, create oxygen vacancies and promote C–C coupling. — [OpenAlex, 2026-09-26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.117202) (abstract only)

*Abstract only; stability, full product distribution and energy efficiency not stated.*

### NiTe@C catalyst reaches 91.8% CO Faradaic efficiency in acidic CO2 electrolysis

Researchers report that NiTe@C with a moderately oxidized Ni state achieved 91.8% CO Faradaic efficiency at 300 mA cm−2 in acidic electrolyte and 97.6% at 500 mA cm−2 in neutral electrolyte in a membrane electrode assembly. A cation-immobilized layer between cathode and cation-exchange membrane yielded 70 h stability in acidic electrolyte with 0.05 M H2SO4 and 0.005 M K2SO4, according to the abstract. — [OpenAlex, 2026-10-07](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c11243) (abstract only)

*Abstract only; full-cell voltage and durability beyond 70 h absent.*

### Two-microbe route upgrades CO2 electrolysis products to C4–C6 carboxylates

A University of Delaware study reports a two-step microbial route that upgrades C1–C2 electrocatalytic CO2 reduction products to C4–C6 carboxylates: C. ljungdahlii biofilms convert formate to acetate, and C. kluyveri elongates acetate and ethanol to butyrate, caproate and other medium-chain carboxylates. The authors report C. kluyveri tolerated formate up to 50 mM and C. ljungdahlii biofilms converted up to 50 mM formate in CO2RR-type phosphate solutions, according to bioengineer.org. — [bioengineer.org, 2026-10-06](https://bioengineer.org/microbe-team-turns-co2-electrolysis-waste-into-valuable-chemicals) (news report)

*Demonstrated with reconstituted mixtures, not industrial electrolyzer effluent; no product titers.*

## Economics, climate & policy

### Review catalogs CCU pathways, LCA ranges, barriers and policy gaps

A Frontiers in Climate review by Nosherwani et al. surveys CO2 utilization routes and estimates current global CO2 utilization at about 250 Mt/y in 2023, with urea at 130 Mt/y (~52%) and EOR at 80 Mt/y (~32%). It reports selected LCA ranges: mineral carbonation GWP 524–1,073 kg CO2-eq/tCO2 removed; methanol −0.325 tCO2-eq/t; PtG/PtX below 100 g CO2-eq/kWh; CO2-derived PE/PP 0.006–6 kg CO2-eq versus 0.02–9 kg for fossil. — [OpenAlex, 2026-10-05](https://doi.org/10.3389/fclim.2026.1809218)

The review identifies CO2 transport as a major financial hurdle, and material-based CCU as constrained by technological immaturity, high energy demand and competitiveness against conventional products. It stresses that most CCU technologies have not been tested at commercial scales and that LCA net benefits depend strongly on renewable electricity. — [OpenAlex, 2026-10-05](https://doi.org/10.3389/fclim.2026.1809218)

*Review synthesis; LCA values not directly comparable due to differing assumptions.*
